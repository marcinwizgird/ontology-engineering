# Context beats models across NL2Query

As of 19 October 2026, natural-language-to-query works well wherever the semantics are already written down, and poorly wherever they are not. Clean public Text-to-SQL is saturated or close to it: Spider 1.0 has sat around 91% execution accuracy since 2023–24, and BIRD's best test score is 82.95% against 92.96% for humans. But these numbers are measured on benchmarks where a 2026 audit found more than half the gold labels wrong. Realistic tasks score far lower: 15–50% on enterprise-knowledge benchmarks, 17% for GPT-5 on interactive BIRD-Interact, and roughly 0% on BEAVER's real warehouses. Text-to-SPARQL has become a set of small shared tasks (50–200 hidden questions each). On TEXT2SPARQL 2026 the winner scored set-F1 0.72–0.85, and the deciding lever was synthetic, execution-filtered example generation, not the agent architecture. Text-to-Cypher evaluation is still immature. The most reliable gains come from three things: curated semantics in context (+16 to +40 points), value or entity grounding, and execution-guided repair with good candidate selection. Constrained decoding, many-agent pipelines and tight schema pruning show little or no measured value with frontier models. Every head-to-head number for ontology or semantic-layer approaches comes from one small, vendor-run insurance benchmark (11–43 questions). For the FIBO virtual knowledge graph (VKG), the design in [NL2SPARQL-ASSESSMENT.md](../../../LAB/PoCs/SPARQL-2-SQL/NL2SPARQL-ASSESSMENT.md) holds up. The 2026 evidence adds five things to it: a synthetic example bank built from the mapped vocabulary, a generic tool-using agent as a mandatory baseline, an "abstain on everything" floor, contamination probes (FIBO is public), and paired statistics over repeated runs.

## Public Text-to-SQL leaderboards saturated while realistic tasks stayed below 50%

The Text-to-SQL frontier has split in two. **Spider 1.0 is saturated at about 91% EX**: MiniSeek reported 91.2%, though one curated list records 91.6, so even that figure is disputed ([Seek AI](https://seek.ai/blog/miniseek-first-model-to-surpass-90-accuracy-on-spider-test-benchmark); [Spider 2.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763)). On **BIRD**, the leaderboard fetched on 2026-10-19 shows GrainSQL at **82.95 test EX** (dated 2026-09-26). Behind it come Huawei's DataGallery at 82.39 and Tencent's SiriusAI-SQL at 82.28. All three are leaderboard-only entries with no paper. The best entry with a paper is Ant Group's Agentar-Scale-SQL at **81.67** ([BIRD](https://bird-bench.github.io/); [arXiv 2509.24403](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24403)). That is roughly 9 points short of the human 92.96, after only about 1–2 points of progress a year at the top since late 2024.

**The Spider 2.0 numbers need careful dating, because the notes disagree and the leaderboard has no per-entry dates.** Four points make the history readable. First, the widely quoted 21.3% for an o1-preview agent comes from the original 632-task agentic "Spider2" setting, which was removed on 2025-05-22 ([arXiv 2411.07763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07763); [leaderboard](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)). It is therefore not a baseline for Lite, and a "21 → 76" trajectory compares different task sets. The site's own baselines for the current settings are Spider-Agent + o1-preview at 23.58 (Snow) and 23.03 (Lite), and ReFoRCE + o3 reached 35.83 and 36.56 in 2025 ([arXiv 2502.00675](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00675)). Second, a third-party snapshot from June 2026 put the Lite top at **52.9%**, a single vendor's result ([BenchLM](https://www.benchlm.ai/benchmarks/spider2Lite)). Third, the official page read on 2026-10-19 lists Tencent's Tianqiong Data Agent + GLM 5.2 at **76.23**, ahead of DecisionX (74.95), Kaelio's ktx (73.67) and Snowflake/UCSD's DivSkill-SQL (73.13). Fourth, there is no way to tell whether those entries were added between June and October or whether the snapshot simply lagged. The defensible statement is: "Lite best rose from about 37 (published, 2025) to at least 53 (third-party, June 2026), and the leaderboard listed 76 on 19 October 2026, self-reported and undated." A generic coding agent, Claude Code + Sonnet, is listed at 61.2 on Lite, already well above every 2025 research pipeline ([leaderboard](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)).

**Treat any Spider 2.0-Snow score above 90% as unverified.** Gold answers were published on 2024-12-24 and gold tables on 2025-04-20 ([leaderboard](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)). The Snow evaluation account was suspended in August 2026 ([Spider2 repo](https://github.com/xlang-ai/Spider2)). An expert audit found that **62.8% of the auditable Snow examples have wrong gold**; the CIDR version of the paper gives 66.1% over 121 problems ([arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778)). A system that matches wrong gold on 96.7% of tasks has, by implication, learned the benchmark's errors. One caveat limits that inference: the audit covered only the 121 examples with public gold, not all 547. Still, a knowledge-graph-grounded entry (AT&T Ask Data with a RelationalAI knowledge graph, 86.28 on Snow) sits among the leaders. That is weak but real evidence that ontology grounding competes at the frontier.

Reasoning models and reinforcement learning moved the numbers in specific places. **Reinforcement learning with execution-only rewards (GRPO), applied to open coder models already fine-tuned on synthetic data, brought single 32B models to 71.83 BIRD test EX** with greedy decoding (Arctic-Text2SQL-R1, ACL Findings 2026). Most of the gap between open and closed models has closed ([arXiv 2505.20315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20315)). These specialised models do not transfer to enterprise SQL, though: they score 10–16% on Spider 2.0-SQLite against GPT-4o's 14.8 ([arXiv 2503.02240](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02240v2)). On contamination-resistant and interactive tasks, frontier models cluster at **36–41% success on LiveSQLBench** (Claude Opus 5.5 inside Jitto Build at 41.33, Opus 4.6 at 38.00, Gemini 3.1 Pro at 36.50). GPT-5 manages 8.67% in conversational and 17.00% in agentic BIRD-Interact ([LiveSQLBench](https://livesqlbench.ai/); [arXiv 2510.05318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05318)). In a 50-case error analysis, more than 80% of BIRD-Interact failures came from incomplete ambiguity resolution. Systems asked about once per task, while tasks contain about four ambiguities. Enterprise benchmarks fail for the same reasons. Falcon (Ant Group, MaxCompute dialect) caps state-of-the-art models at 50%, with errors concentrated in schema linking and in mapping colloquial phrasing to exact aggregations and time windows ([arXiv 2510.24762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24762)). The gap is about knowledge and context, not SQL syntax.

## SPARQL and Cypher run on small challenges with incompatible metrics

Knowledge-graph question answering (KGQA) has moved from legacy corpora to hidden-test shared tasks. **TEXT2SPARQL 2026** (ESWC, Dubrovnik) tested eight approaches on two sets. One is 50 DBpedia questions (EN+ES). The other is 50 questions (EN+DE) over a private "corporate" knowledge graph of 13 classes, 30 properties and about 27k triples. Scoring is set-based F1, plus nDCG for ordered answers ([challenge](https://text2sparql.aksw.org/latest/participation/challenge/)). **SPARQL-LLM (SIB) won**, according to its authors' own paper. Its F1 against the runner-up ARUQULA was **0.72 vs 0.61 (DBpedia EN)**, **0.78 vs 0.49 (DBpedia ES)** and **0.85 vs 0.64 (Corporate EN)**. It ran GPT-5.2 at temperature 0 for under $3 in total ([arXiv 2610.13648](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13648)). The journal version reports up to 27× lower latency (3.1 s median against 112.5 s for the 2025 winner) and about $0.01 per question ([arXiv 2512.14277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14277)). **This is weak evidence**: no official per-team leaderboard was found, the 2025 winner is disputed between ARUQULA and mKGQAgent ([arXiv 2507.16971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16971v1)), and with 50 questions per track a few F1 points are noise.

The mechanism behind the win matters more than the margin. **SPARQL-LLM synthesised question–query pairs for the private graph and kept only those that returned results on the endpoint.** It then retrieved them as few-shot examples, alongside VoID/ShEx schema summaries and a validator that checks every triple pattern against the schema ([arXiv 2512.14277](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14277v1)). With this example bank, corporate scores *exceeded* DBpedia scores. The usual gap between public and private graphs disappears once examples exist for the private graph. ARUQULA's exploration-heavy ReAct agent took 51–66 s and 8–11 tool steps per question and finished about 20 F1 points behind on the corporate set ([arXiv 2510.02200](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02200v1)).

On Wikidata, **WikiKGQA 2026** (ISWC, 150 hidden questions, Macro QALD-F1) was led by agentic systems. ASTER (IBM) scored 0.88 and AMA-KBQA 0.87 on English questions with entity mentions supplied; GRASP/GRISP scored 0.85 without mentions ([WikiKGQA overview](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4284/intro1.pdf)). The organizers report two caveats. Off-the-shelf **Claude-based baselines were "competitive with KGQA-specific approaches"**. And answers can be reverse-engineered from parametric knowledge of Wikidata, the test set contains no OPTIONAL, and scores cluster in [0.8, 0.9) because hard questions are scarce. Generic agents' advantage on Wikidata therefore partly reflects memorised identifiers, which do not transfer to a private ontology. Legacy benchmarks are now training material at best. A 2025 audit of 16 KGQA datasets found inaccurate and outdated gold ([arXiv 2505.23495](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23495v4)). Many LC-QuAD 2.0 queries no longer return answers on live Wikidata ([arXiv 2204.12793](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.12793)). Spider4SPARQL, the large conversion of Spider's relational databases into knowledge graphs, capped LLMs at **45% execution accuracy** ([arXiv 2309.16248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16248)).

**Text-to-Cypher has the least mature evaluation.** CypherBench (Wikidata-derived property graphs) uses execution accuracy. On it, Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored 61.58 and GPT-4o 60.18, while models under 10B parameters scored below 20%. These are older-generation figures from a secondary summary ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2412.18702v2)). Neo4j's 44k-instance Text2Cypher corpus is scored with Google-BLEU and stringified exact match, and Neo4j itself admits a stray newline breaks the match ([Neo4j](https://neo4j.com/blog/developer/benchmarking-neo4j-text2cypher-dataset/)). IBM's Mind the Query (27.5k validated pairs) and Text2GQL-Bench (ISO GQL via an intermediate representation) add data but no accepted semantic metric ([IBM](https://researchweb.draco.res.ibm.com/publications/mind-the-query-a-benchmark-dataset-towards-text2cypher-task); [arXiv 2602.11745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11745v1)).

These metrics cannot be compared with one another. TEXT2SPARQL's set-F1, WikiKGQA's Macro QALD-F1 (macro precision and recall first, then F1), CypherBench and Spider4SPARQL's exact-result execution accuracy, and Neo4j's BLEU/EM measure different things. A 0.85 F1 is not "better" than 61.58 EX. **No KGQA test set reports confidence intervals.** The only published evaluation of ontology-based data access with LLMs (OWL + R2RML over 13 insurance tables) predates 2025. **No 2025–26 paper evaluates LLM-generated SPARQL executed through Ontop, and no FIBO NL→SPARQL benchmark exists** ([arXiv 2311.07509](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.07509)).

## Grounding and repair beat clever generation in the ablation record

Three conclusions follow. First, **give a frontier model as much relevant semantics as it can read rather than pruning tightly.** In the "Death of Schema Linking" study, every schema-linking variant lowered accuracy for all three models, because recall losses outweighed the noise removed ([arXiv 2408.07702](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07702v2)). Google found little degradation from irrelevant tables and no "lost in the middle" effect, though latency becomes high and variable beyond about 32k tokens ([arXiv 2501.12372](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12372)). Pruning still pays for small models and for schemas that do not fit in context. Second, **relevance beats volume in examples.** One ground-truth example lifts Gemini to about 78%, while a hundred generic ones add under a point. This explains why CHASE-SQL's instance-aware synthetic examples and SPARQL-LLM's endpoint-filtered synthetic examples were each system's strongest generation lever. Third, **the selection and repair stage is where the remaining headroom sits.** The roughly 10-point gap between the best candidate and the selected one in CHASE-SQL is larger than any generator's marginal contribution inside the ensemble (0.6–1.2 pp).

Graph query languages repeat these patterns, but with thinner evidence. Entity and IRI linking plays the role of value retrieval and is repeatedly named the dominant error source. A 2024 baseline reached 62.7% exact match at 3-shot when given gold entity linking ([arXiv 2402.00969](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.00969v1)). The same systems drop when gold mentions are withdrawn on WikiKGQA (ASTER 0.88 → 0.81), although the two question sets are disjoint. Ontology quality also matters. **In one small study (21 competency questions, local models), readable names and annotations outweighed model choice and prompt engineering** ([arXiv 2607.18029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18029)). An ontology-based query check (OBQC) with LLM repair raised insurance-graph accuracy to 72% with 8% "I don't know" ([arXiv 2405.11706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706)). **No paper on SPARQL or Cypher has a CHASE-style component ablation, and none isolates constrained decoding or reinforcement learning with execution rewards at frontier scale.** Two weaker cautions also appear. Outside SQL, a 7B study found format and implementation details mattered more than multi-agent architecture, with JSON message passing far worse than plaintext ([arXiv 2607.26922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26922)). And dbt measured no accuracy gain from higher reasoning effort, despite queries taking more than 20 s instead of 8 s ([dbt](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026)).

## More than half the gold labels are wrong, so evaluation methodology decides results

The most consequential 2026 finding concerns the yardstick, not any system. **Jin, Choi, Zhu and Kang (CIDR 2026 / PVLDB 19) found annotation errors in 52.8% of BIRD Mini-Dev (263 of 498) and 62.8% of auditable Spider 2.0-Snow examples (76 of 121).** The dominant category is gold SQL that does not match the actual data or schema. When 16 open-source agents were re-scored on a corrected BIRD Dev subset, ranks moved by up to ±9 places and Contextual-SQL fell from 1st to 7th. Rank correlation with the full Dev set fell from Spearman 0.85 (uncorrected subset) to **0.32** (corrected subset, p=0.23) ([arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08778v3)). The audit itself has limits. It reports no inter-annotator kappa, it relied on an o3 agent whose flags were 83–89% precise, and the CIDR and arXiv versions report different headline examples. Even so, gaps of under about 3 points at the top of BIRD are not meaningful, and leaderboard rank should not drive model selection.

Metric choice compounds the problem. Spider's exact-set match has a 2.5% average false-negative rate (8.1% worst case). Single-database EX accepts queries that are wrong but coincidentally return the right rows, and test suites of distilled databases fix this at a compute cost ([Zhong et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.29)). **Evaluating on several synthesised database instances lowers scores by 3–14% across 10 models on Spider, BIRD and Spider 2.0.** These instances are generated from the question and schema rather than from the gold query ([arXiv 2604.27261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27261)). LLM judges agree with human experts far better than EX does: FLEX reaches Cohen's kappa 0.87 against 0.62 for EX ([NAACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.228)). But judges are probabilistic and sensitive to temperature. Formal equivalence checkers are sound but cover little: SQLSolver supports about 30% of query pairs and VeriEQL 2.8% ([arXiv 2412.05561](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05561v2)). For SPARQL, answer-set F1 is the community standard. T2S-Metrics bundles more than 20 metrics, but **no human-agreement study exists for any SPARQL metric** ([arXiv 2604.26971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26971)).

Benchmarks that target trustworthiness show how much clean scores hide. On Dr.Spider's 17 perturbations, the most robust model loses 14% overall and 50.7% on the hardest perturbation ([arXiv 2301.08881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08881)). AmbiQT and AMBROSIA show that LLMs rarely recognise ambiguity. Most sobering, **under TrustSQL's penalty scoring, no method consistently beat the trivial baseline of abstaining on every question** ([arXiv 2403.15879](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15879v4)). Contamination is measurable. SPENCE's paraphrase-decay test (ACL 2026) finds strong leakage signals on Spider while BIRD looks "largely uncontaminated" ([arXiv 2604.17771](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17771)). GPT-3.5 drops on the deliberately unseen Termite set ([arXiv 2402.08100](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08100v1)). Outputs also vary across runs at temperature 0 (Uber reports about 5% run-to-run variance), yet leaderboards report a single run ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/en-us/blog/query-gpt/)). Good 2026 papers have converged on a statistical norm: **paired comparisons using exact McNemar plus paired bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples), with discordant-pair counts reported** ([arXiv 2608.22651](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22651); [arXiv 2609.00834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00834)). At 70% accuracy, a single-system 95% interval is about ±4 points with 500 items.

## Curated semantics lift accuracy, but the ontology evidence is small and vendor-run

**All head-to-head evidence on semantic-layer and ontology approaches comes from one small family: data.world's ACME insurance benchmark (11–43 questions), mostly run by vendors.** In 2023, GPT-4 scored 16.7% querying SQL directly and 54.2% querying an OWL + R2RML knowledge graph ([arXiv 2311.07509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07509)). Adding OBQC and repair gave 72% correct, 8% "I don't know" and about 20% wrong ([arXiv 2405.11706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11706)). In August 2026, semantic path compilation (SPC) reported 37/38 questions correct on all three runs (97.4%), against 21/38 for direct DDL-to-SQL, with McNemar p≈3×10⁻⁵ and **zero wrong-but-executed runs**. SPC lets the model choose only among governed options; code then builds paths, grain and SQL. Its author calls this a systems claim, not a causal one, and the paper is promoted by an affiliated company blog ([arXiv 2608.16663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16663)). On the semantic-layer side, dbt's 2026 update (11 questions × 20 runs) is the most informative because it reports its own limits. On the original schema, frontier text-to-SQL rose to 64.5% and the semantic layer to 72.7%. **The semantic layer scored 0% on questions needing too many entity hops, which its metric layer, MetricFlow, cannot express, while text-to-SQL got 70–100% on them.** After three extra dbt models were added, the scores were 90.0/84.1% (text-to-SQL) against 98.2/100% (semantic layer) ([dbt](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026)). Cube found that even an informal 4 KB markdown description adds +17 to +23 pp across Opus 4.7, Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4, with no significant difference between models once semantics were supplied ([arXiv 2604.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25149)).

Read together, these results lead to an uncomfortable conclusion: **most of the gain comes from having curated semantics at all, not from their formalism.** No independent study shows ontologies beating a well-modelled semantic layer, or the reverse. Cross-paper comparisons such as "semantic layer 83% vs knowledge graph 54%" mix a favourable subset with a 2023 zero-shot run ([dbt 2023](https://www.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-as-the-data-interface-for-llms)). What formal semantics demonstrably change is the failure mode. OBQC's abstentions, SPC's zero silent errors and dbt's "errors, not wrong data" all show checkable semantics turning plausible wrong numbers into refusals. The one pre-LLM data point on FIBO is IBM's ATHENA++, which reached 88.33% on FIBEN (300 questions, 170 nested) over a FIBO/FRO-grounded schema ([PVLDB 13](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol13/p2747-sen.pdf); [FIBEN](https://github.com/IBM/fiben-benchmark)). Marketing claims such as Stardog's "100% hallucination-free", Snowflake Cortex Analyst's "90%+" on an internal benchmark of flattened tables, and AtScale's "100%" against reused data.world baselines carry no independent weight ([Snowflake](https://snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi); [Atlan on Stardog](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/knowledge-graph/stardog-vs-atlan.md)).

Production reports measure productivity, not autonomous correctness. Uber's QueryGPT cut query authoring from about 10 to about 3 minutes for about 300 daily users, but hallucinated tables remain "not fully solved" ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/en-us/blog/query-gpt/)). **LinkedIn's SQL Bot has 300+ weekly users and 53% of answers rated correct or close by experts**. Its "knowledge graph" is a retrieval index over metadata, query logs, wikis and code, not an ontology ([arXiv 2507.14372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14372)). Every successful deployment is a copilot for SQL-literate users, built on a curated, domain-scoped context store whose maintenance is the main cost. Genie partners gate promotion at 60/70/80% on curated benchmarks ([Koantek](https://www.koantek.com/blog-posts/productizing-databricks-genie-at-enterprise-scale)). The operational risks are documented. Prompt-to-SQL injection (P2SQL, ICSE 2025) bypasses prompt-level restrictions and can only be stopped at the database permission and query-guard layer ([arXiv 2308.01990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01990)). Snowflake itself lists metric-definition drift (substituting a different forecast-error metric) and unstated temporal assumptions as recurring failures. No vendor publishes accuracy measured on real usage, and no deployment publishes accuracy drift over time.

## What the evidence means for the FIBO VKG layer

The assessment's core architecture survives the 2026 evidence: an ontology card limited to mapped terms, OBQC-style validation, a bounded intermediate representation (IR) with a deterministic renderer, test-suite EX across perturbed DuckDB instances, wrong-but-executed as the headline risk metric, and FIBEN and ACME as external sets. The evidence above sharpens it in several places. Where the notes framed recommendations around Fuseki, they translate directly to the Ontop setting. "Pin the triple-store snapshot" becomes pinning the DuckDB seed, the R2RML mapping and the ontology version together. "Perturb named graphs" becomes perturbing the relational instances, which Ontop then exposes unchanged. "Execute against the endpoint" becomes compile-then-run on DuckDB, with the compiler's 422 responses as a free validity signal.

**First, make an execution-filtered synthetic example bank a primary lever, not just a few-shot pool.** SPARQL-LLM's private-graph result and CHASE-SQL's +9.3 pp both point here. The bank must be generated from the *mapped* vocabulary. Under Ontop, an unmapped property returns an empty result, and an "execution-guided" non-empty filter would wrongly treat it as a bad query. The assessment's gold "cannot answer" items should stay separate from the example bank for the same reason. **Second, add a fifth arm: a generic tool-using agent** (frontier model plus tools for ontology lookup, compile and execute, without the IR). Claude Code was competitive on WikiKGQA and scores 61.2 on Spider 2.0-Lite, so any claim for the bespoke arms must beat it. FIBO is public, though, so its advantage may partly be memorisation (see the fifth point). **Third, report an "abstain on everything" floor** in the TrustSQL penalty score at c∈{1,5}. Published detectors failed to beat it, and the ontology's claim to value is precisely refusal quality.

**Fourth, prefer recall-oriented context over pruning when the full FIBO grows.** The schema-linking evidence says to keep the whole mapped card in context while it fits (under about 30k tokens, cached). Prune by mapped terms first, and only then by retrieval. Measure entity and value linking separately, since it is the dominant error class in both SQL and SPARQL. **Fifth, control for contamination explicitly.** FIBO and FIBEN are on the public web. Add a schema-renaming probe (opaque IRIs and labels) and SPENCE-style paraphrase decay to E0, and report the drop. **Sixth, adopt the 2026 statistical norm**: at least 3 runs per item, solid success (k/k), exact McNemar with discordant counts, and paired bootstrap intervals. Calibrate any LLM judge against a human-labelled sample and report kappa, with FLEX's 0.87 as the bar. Mine real query logs for evaluation and examples where they exist; LinkedIn and Uber found them to be the highest-signal context. Enforce access control under the end user's identity in the DuckDB/Ontop connection, never in the prompt.

Two claims should stay modest. **No published system evaluates LLM-generated SPARQL through Ontop, and no FIBO NL→SPARQL benchmark exists**, so this PoC's numbers would be novel, but they cannot be benchmarked against a direct precedent. ACME (E1) gives the only literature comparability, and it runs on 38–43 questions. That is enough to detect only very large paired differences. And because frontier text-to-SQL now reaches 64–90% on small schemas, arm T (raw text-to-SQL) is a serious competitor. The ontology layer's most defensible ground is multi-hop, role- and path-sensitive FIBO questions, together with abstention and silent-error rates. Raw accuracy on simple lookups is unlikely to separate it from arm T.

## Conclusion

The field's centre of gravity has moved from generating queries to grounding and checking them. The 2024–26 gains on hard benchmarks came from agents exploring context and from models trained against execution. The gains that survive on enterprise-like data came from someone writing the semantics down. The yardsticks themselves turned out to be half wrong. A leaderboard rank is therefore weak evidence; a paired, multi-instance, repeated-run evaluation on your own schema is strong evidence. Few groups have built one, which makes it the most valuable contribution available.

For the FIBO VKG, the practical upshot is a measurement programme more than a model choice. It should test whether OWL plus R2RML buys anything beyond "curated semantics" through three numbers: the wrong-but-executed rate, refusal quality against the abstain-everything floor, and accuracy on multi-hop questions. Each should be compared against both raw text-to-SQL and a generic tool-using agent. If the ontology arm wins on silent errors and multi-hop questions but ties on lookups, that is a publishable and honest result. Nobody has yet shown it independently.
